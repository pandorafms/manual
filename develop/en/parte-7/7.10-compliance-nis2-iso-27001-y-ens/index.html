    Compliance: NIS2, ISO 27001 and ENS¶

    A green dashboard is not a declaration of conformity. It may mean that monitoring works, that agents are reporting and that nobody has touched what they should not have; or it may mean that the dashboard has been looking the other way for six months. The difference between the two is not the colour of the widgets, but the ability to answer an uncomfortable question: "show me that this is met".

    Pandora FMS does not sign policies, train staff or answer to a certification body. What it does do is produce data, detections and evidence that an organisation can use to support its compliance programme. This chapter is about that: which capability serves which article, control or measure, and how to turn that relationship into evidence that survives an audit.

    It is not a summary of NIS2, ISO/IEC 27001:2022 or ENS. It is a mapping table. Only the regulatory points with an associated capability appear; the rest of the regulation is left out because it cannot be resolved with a monitoring tool.

    • What can be met is the second important column. It explains what the tool covers and what remains outside: policy, decision, training, supplier or signature.
    • Evidence is what gets exported. If it cannot be repeated, dated and retained, it is not useful in an audit.
    • References are deliberately brief. The article or control is not transcribed: it is only identified. The detail lives in the standard.

    The table does not include articles without an associated capability. Nor does it include ENS measures that are purely organisational or ISO 27001 clauses that are resolved with documents, minutes or management decisions. If a requirement is not there, it is not an oversight: it cannot be met or tracked with Pandora FMS.

    The standards in one line¶

    ISO/IEC 27001:2022¶

    ISO/IEC 27001:2022 is a management system standard, not a product catalogue. The table keeps clauses 9.1, 9.2 and 9.3 —monitoring, internal audit and management review— and the Annex A controls with an associated capability. The real control selection comes from the risk analysis and is justified in the statement of applicability; Pandora FMS helps feed and demonstrate it, not decide it.

    ENS¶

    The Spanish National Security Framework is regulated by Royal Decree 311/2022. The table covers the Annex II measures that can be met or tracked with Pandora FMS, especially those in the operational framework and those protecting communications, information and services. Organisational measures (org.*), people measures (mp.per.*), facilities measures (mp.if.*) and secure development measures (mp.sw.*) are left out: they are documented and evidenced elsewhere.

    NIS2¶

    Directive (EU) 2022/2555 requires essential and important entities to manage risk, involve their management body and report significant incidents. Only three articles make it into the table: 20 (governance and oversight), 21 (cybersecurity risk-management measures) and 23 (reporting). Article 23 sets an early warning within 24 hours, a notification within 72 hours and a final report within one month of that notification.

    NIS2 transposition is not the same in every country

    NIS2 is a directive and each Member State incorporates it into its own legal system. In Spain, at the time of this edition (November 2026), the transposition process is still pending and the NIS1 framework remains in force. Before using this table in an audit, confirm the applicable national law.

    Four use cases¶

    A sample for ISO 27001¶

    The auditor asks for a sample of controls. The answer should not be a ZIP full of screenshots. It should be a package with inventory, permission report, vulnerability evolution, hardening index, backup history, restore test, audit events and change tickets. Each piece with its date, filter and owner.

    The table says which capability feeds each control. The audit work starts afterwards: checking that the sample is representative, that the period is right and that exceptions are justified.

    An ENS audit¶

    For ENS, the table is read by measure. op.exp.1 is evidenced with the inventory; op.exp.8, with the audit log and logs; op.exp.2, op.exp.3 and op.exp.5, with NCM and the change inventory; op.mon.2, with reports and dashboards; mp.info.6, with backups and their restore. Organisational measures do not come out of the console and should not be presented as if they did.

    A NIS2 incident against the clock¶

    A Pandora FMS SIEM rule or a FIM event detects something that does not add up. The SIEM normalises and correlates the logs; Pandora FMS opens the event with its severity, rule and MITRE reference, tags it, assigns it and can create an ITSM ticket. From there, the timeline is rebuilt with events, OpenSearch logs, the audit log and ticket comments.

    That is what the first 24 hours need: what happened, when and with what impact. For the 72 hours, the initial assessment and indicators of compromise. For the final report, root cause, applied measures and impact. Pandora FMS helps detect, log and export; it does not send the notification to the CSIRT or decide whether the incident is significant. That decision remains human.

    Pandora FMS SIEM and incident response¶

    Pandora FMS SIEM is the piece that turns log noise into something you can investigate. The collector stores the text; the SIEM normalises it, decodes it, applies rules and generates security events. Without that layer, incident response is done with grep and memory; with it, there is a timeline and a list of indicators you can show.

    It is not a decorative add-on: it can act as a corporate SIEM if it covers the sources, retention and correlation the organisation needs. What it does not replace is the team that operates it or the rest of the compliance programme.

    The SIEM relies on log collection in OpenSearch, requires the corresponding licence and is configured with decoders and rules. Each event can carry severity, group, rule, MITRE ATT&CK and the fields from the original log. From there you can alert, open an ITSM ticket and reconstruct what happened.

    What it contributes at each stage¶

    Response stage What Pandora FMS SIEM does Evidence
    Preparation Defines log sources, decoders, rules, severities, retention and ITSM integration Versioned rules, connected sources and event-generation tests
    Detection Normalises and correlates logs; generates SIEM events and triggers alerts SIEM events, alerts and timestamps
    Analysis and triage Supports searching, filtering, contextualising and grouping events; adds MITRE, severity and log fields Queries, detailed events, comments and dashboards
    Containment and response Launches event responses, alert commands, scripts, tickets and escalation Tickets, executed actions and acknowledgements
    Recovery and learning Closes the incident, preserves evidence, reviews rules and tunes detections Post-incident report, rule changes and KPIs

    How it fits the standards¶

    • NIS2, Article 21.2.b. Covers detection and incident handling: collection, correlation, event generation and response.
    • NIS2, Article 23. Provides the timeline, indicators of compromise and evidence for the 24-hour early warning, the 72-hour notification and the final report. It does not send the notification to the CSIRT or decide whether the incident is significant.
    • ISO/IEC 27001:2022. Feeds A.5.7 (threat intelligence), A.5.24–A.5.28 (incident management), A.8.15 (logging) and A.8.16 (monitoring).
    • ENS. Covers op.exp.7 (incident management), op.exp.8 (activity logging), op.exp.9 (incident management logging), op.mon.1 (intrusion detection) and op.mon.3 (surveillance).

    What Pandora FMS does not do¶

    • It does not replace a GRC, an EDR, an IAM or a CMDB.
    • Pandora FMS SIEM can replace a corporate SIEM when it covers the required sources, retention and correlation; what it does not replace is the team that operates it or the rest of the compliance programme.
    • It does not write policies, approve risks or sign declarations.
    • It does not train staff or manage supplier contracts.
    • It does not notify incidents to the competent authority.
    • It does not by itself guarantee compliance with NIS2, ISO 27001 or ENS.
    • It does not cover ENS organisational measures or ISO 27001 leadership, planning and improvement clauses.

    Confusing these things is the quickest way to turn a useful tool into a broken promise. The table tells you what can be demonstrated with Pandora FMS and what has to be resolved elsewhere.
